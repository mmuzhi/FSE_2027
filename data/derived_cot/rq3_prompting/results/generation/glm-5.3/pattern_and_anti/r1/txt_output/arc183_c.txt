Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** Count via interval DP on the position of the maximum. For interval $[l,r]$, if the max is at $m$, then any condition with $[L,R]\subseteq[l,r]$ and $L\le m\le R$ has its interval-max at $m$ — so it's satisfied iff $X\ne m$. Conditions fully inside $[l,m-1]$ or $[m+1,r]$ recurse; interleaving values gives a binomial factor:

$$f(l,r)=\sum_{m:\,\nexists\, i,\, X_i=m,\, l\le L_i,\, R_i\le r}\binom{r-l}{m-l}f(l,m-1)f(m+1,r)$$

This is $O(N^3)$, which for $N=500$ needs numpy vectorization: loop over interval length, vectorize over $(l, d=m-l)$ grids, with precomputed $B[x][r]=\max\{L_i: X_i=x, R_i\le r\}$ (forbidden iff $B[m][r]\ge l$). Overflow is controlled by reducing mod before summing (each term $<p$, sum over $\le 500$ terms $<2^{63}$).

I verified the recurrence on samples 1 and 2 by hand (both give the correct answers, including the always-violated $(x,x,x)$ case yielding 0), plus small $N=2$ cases.
