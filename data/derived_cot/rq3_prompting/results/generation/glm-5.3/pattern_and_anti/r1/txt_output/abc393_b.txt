=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- |S| ≤ 100, so even O(n²) brute force is trivially fast.
- **Plan**: For each position `j` where S[j] == 'B', try every gap `d` and check whether `S[j-d] == 'A'` and `S[j+d] == 'C'` (with bounds respected). Count matches.
- **Invariant**: every valid triple (i, j, k) is uniquely determined by its center j and gap d = j - i = k - j, so iterating over (j, d) counts each triple exactly once.
- **Edge check**: as `d` grows, `i` shrinks and `k` grows, so once out of bounds we can `break`.

Verification on Sample 1 ("AABCC"): B at index 2; d=1 gives (1,3), d=2 gives (1,5 as A,C) → 2 triples ✓. Sample 3 ("AABAAABBAEDCCCD") also yields 4 by manual walk (2 triples centered at index 6, 2 at index 7) ✓.
