=== ANSWER ===
**Approach.** Binary search on the answer `w`. For feasibility, each process independently needs minimum cost to reach capacity ≥ `w` using unlimited copies of machines (A,P) and (B,Q); the sum of these minima must be ≤ X.

For a single process, in some optimal solution either `x < B` (units of S) or `y < A` (units of T): if both were large, replacing B copies of S by A copies of T (or vice versa) keeps capacity identical and changes cost by ±(AQ−BP), one direction of which is ≤ 0. So enumerate `x ∈ [0, B−1]` with minimal `y = ceil((w−xA)/B)`, and symmetrically `y ∈ [0, A−1]` with minimal `x`. Since A, B ≤ 100 this is ≤ 200 candidates per process per check.

Bound: X ≤ 10⁷, prices ≥ 1, capacities ≤ 100 ⇒ answer ≤ 10⁹. Binary search over [0, 10⁹]: ~30 iterations × 100 processes × 200 candidates ≈ 6·10⁵ operations — fast.

Verified on samples 1–3 by hand (e.g. sample 1: costs 10+4+8=22 for w=4, w=5 needs 27 > 22 → answer 4; sample 2 gives exactly 10⁹; sample 3 gives 0). Edge case w=0 handled since x=0,y=0 costs 0.

def main():
