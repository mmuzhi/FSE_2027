=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- **Target complexity:** O(N) — just a linear walk through the points summing Euclidean distances (origin → P1 → ... → PN → origin).
- **Precision:** Python ints make dx²+dy² exact (up to ~8×10^18, no overflow in Python). Converting to float for `sqrt` gives relative error ~2⁻⁵² per segment; summed over 2×10⁵ segments, total relative error ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ at worst — far within the 10⁻⁶ tolerance.
- **Output:** print with 20 decimal places (matches sample style, well beyond required tolerance).

Key invariant: after processing point k, `total` = distance origin→P1→…→Pk and `(px,py)` = Pk; final step adds the return leg to origin. Edge case N=1 (out and back = 2·dist) falls out naturally from the loop structure.

Verified against Sample 1 (2.2360679… + 2.8284271… + 1 ≈ 6.0644951) and Sample 3 (2·141421.356 + 4·282842.712 ≈ 1414213.562) — both match.
