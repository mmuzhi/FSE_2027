=== ANSWER ===
**Commit:** Brute force is decisive here — N, D ≤ 100 means O(N·D) ≤ 10⁴ weight computations. For each k in 1..D, the answer is max over snakes of T_i·(L_i+k). No data structure or precomputation is needed.

**Ledger:**
- Invariant: weight after growth k is T·(L+k); heaviest snake maximizes this.
- Edge case: single snake (sample 2) — handled naturally by `max`.
- Output: one line per k, in order.

**Recheck against samples:**
- Sample 1, k=1: 3·4=12, 5·2=10, 2·5=10, 1·11=11 → 12 ✓; k=2 → 15 ✓; k=3 → 20 ✓.
- Sample 2: 100·101, 100·102, 100·103, 100·104 → 10100..10400 ✓.

```python
def main():
    import sys
    data = sys.stdin.read().split()
    n, d = int(data[0]), int(data[1])
    snakes = [(int(data[2 + 2 * i]), int(data[3 + 2 * i])) for i in range(n)]
    out = []
    for k in range(1, d + 1):
        out.append(str(max(t * (l + k) for t, l in snakes)))
    print('\n'.join(out))

main()
```
