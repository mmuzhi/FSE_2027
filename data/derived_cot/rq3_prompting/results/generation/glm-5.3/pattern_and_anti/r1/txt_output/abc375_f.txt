**Approach (offline reverse processing):**

- N ≤ 300 → Floyd–Warshall (O(N³)) with numpy is cheap.
- Deletions are hard, insertions are easy: process queries in **reverse**. Start from the graph with *all roads that ever get closed* removed, run Floyd–Warshall. Then scanning queries backward, a type-1 query becomes "insert road i", which updates the full distance matrix in O(N²) via `d_new[u][v] = min(d[u][v], d[u][a]+c+d[b][v], d[u][b]+c+d[a][v])` (valid since positive weights ⇒ the new edge appears at most once in a shortest path). Type-2 queries just read `d[x][y]`.
- Answers are recorded during the reverse scan and printed in forward order. Total: O(N³ + K·N² + Q), K ≤ 300.
- Use int64 with INF = 2⁶⁰ (sums stay < 2⁶³).

Verified against both samples (10/11/−1 and −1/−1/−1).
