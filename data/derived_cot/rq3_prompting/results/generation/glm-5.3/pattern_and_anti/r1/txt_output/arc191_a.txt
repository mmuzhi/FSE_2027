Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** Operation M (writing T[M]=v) is the last write wherever it lands, so exactly one position must end with character v. Every feasible final string assigns distinct chips from multiset T[1..M-1] (optional) plus one mandatory v-chip, and some position must equal v.

**Algorithm:** Run the relaxed greedy F⁺ using the *entire* multiset T as the pool (place the current max pool digit at position i iff it strictly exceeds S[i]; the max is non-increasing over time). If v appears in F⁺, then F⁺ is feasible (the mandatory chip can always be attached/relabelled at any position whose final char is v) and it's optimal since it's the lex-max of a relaxation. If v does not appear in F⁺, then every position of F⁺ is > v, and deferring the mandatory sacrifice to the last position is optimal: answer = F⁺[1..N-1] + v. This follows because any feasible F has a v-position j*, F < F⁺ lexicographically, and the first difference occurs at position ≤ j* where F⁺ (hence the candidate) is strictly larger.

Verified against all three samples (e.g. sample 2: greedy gives "999" with no '3', so answer "993"; sample 3: greedy gives "98888976555" containing '9' ✓) plus edge cases N=1, M=1, and forced-sacrifice scenarios.

Complexity: O(N + 9M) with a tight single pass; the current max digit only decreases (amortized O(9) decreases total), with a sentinel at digit-value 48 for an empty pool.
