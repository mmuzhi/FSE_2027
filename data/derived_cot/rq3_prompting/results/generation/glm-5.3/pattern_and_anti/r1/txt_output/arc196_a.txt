def solve():
    data = sys.stdin.buffer.read().split()

            sumlow += x

Looking at this problem:

**Key structural insights:**

1. The removal process creates a **non-crossing matching** on positions (pairs removed must have had everything between them removed earlier). For odd N, exactly one element is leftover, and crucially, **no removed pair can straddle the leftover** (the leftover is never removed, so a pair containing it could never become adjacent). Thus the leftover splits the sequence into a left part and right part, each perfectly matched.

2. For an even-length segment, the max total of |A_i − A_j| over non-crossing perfect matchings equals the max of Σσ_i A_i over *balanced* sign sequences (Σσ=0), since (a) orienting each pair gives signs summing to 0, and (b) any balanced ±1 sequence admits a non-crossing perfect matching pairing opposite signs (repeatedly pair adjacent opposite signs, which always exists). Hence for even length: **answer = sum(largest half) − sum(smallest half)**.

3. So for odd N: answer = max over leftover at odd 1-indexed position u of G(left part) + G(right part), where G is the even-segment formula. Compute prefix G values and suffix G values incrementally with a two-heap structure (running top-half/bottom-half sums), giving O(N log N).
