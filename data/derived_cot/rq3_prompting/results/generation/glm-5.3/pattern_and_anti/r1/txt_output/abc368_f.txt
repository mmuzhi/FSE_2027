def main():
    data = sys.stdin.buffer.read().split()

    A = list(map(int, data[1:1+n]))

**Analysis:**

Each pile is an independent game where a move replaces $A_i$ with a proper divisor. This is a Sprague–Grundy game; compute Grundy number per pile and XOR.

Claim: the Grundy number of $n$ equals $\Omega(n)$, the number of prime factors of $n$ counted with multiplicity.

Proof by strong induction: any proper divisor $d$ of $n$ has $\Omega(d) < \Omega(n)$, and by removing prime factors one at a time we can achieve a divisor with $\Omega$ value equal to any $j \in \{0, 1, \dots, \Omega(n)-1\}$. By induction the Grundy values of the options are exactly $\{0,\dots,\Omega(n)-1\}$, so the mex is $\Omega(n)$. Base: $g(1)=0=\Omega(1)$.

So Anna wins iff $\bigoplus_i \Omega(A_i) \neq 0$.

Checks: Sample 1: $\Omega = (1,1,2)$, XOR $= 2 \neq 0$ → Anna ✓. Sample 2: $\Omega = (1,1,2,2)$, XOR $= 0$ → Bruno ✓.

Implementation: smallest-prime-factor sieve up to $10^5$, factor each $A_i$ (≤ ~17 factors each), XOR the counts.
