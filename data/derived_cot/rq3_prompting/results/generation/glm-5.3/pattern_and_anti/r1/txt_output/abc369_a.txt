**Commit:** For three numbers to form an arithmetic sequence (in some order), one of them must be the middle term (equal to the average of the other two). With A, B fixed, this gives exactly three candidates for x:

- x is the middle: `x = (A+B)/2` (only if A+B is even, since x must be an integer)
- A is the middle: `A = (B+x)/2` → `x = 2A - B`
- B is the middle: `B = (A+x)/2` → `x = 2B - A`

The answer is the count of **distinct** integer candidates. These coincide only when A = B (all collapse to A).

**Implement:** collect candidates in a set; add the middle-term candidate only when A+B is even; print set size.

- A=5, B=7: {3, 9, 6} → 3 ✓

- A=3, B=3: {3, 3, 3} → 1 ✓ (1,1,1 arithmetic)
- Negative/zero x (e.g. A=1,B=2 → x=0) handled naturally since x just needs to be an integer.

Invariant holds; no edge case breaks it.
