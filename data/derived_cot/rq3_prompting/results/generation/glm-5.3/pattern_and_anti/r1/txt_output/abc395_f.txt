You will be given a competitive programming problem.
Takahashi has 2N teeth: N upper teeth and N lower teeth.
The length of the i-th upper tooth from the left (1 \leq i \leq N) is U _ i, and the length of the i-th lower tooth from the left (1 \leq i \leq N) is D _ i.
His teeth are said to “fit together well” if both of the following conditions are satisfied:

- There exists an integer H such that U _ i + D _ i = H for every integer i with 1 \leq i \leq N.
- \lvert U _ i - U _ {i+1} \rvert \leq X for every integer i with 1 \leq i < N.

He can perform the following operation any number of times:

- Pay 1 yen to use a tooth-grinding machine, choose exactly one tooth whose length is positive, and reduce its length by 1.

No other method may be used to change the lengths of the teeth.
Find the minimum total amount of money he needs to pay to make his teeth fit together well.

Looking at this problem:

- Let final lengths be u_i ≤ U_i, d_i ≤ D_i with u_i + d_i = H. Total cost = Σ(U_i + D_i) − N·H, so we want the **maximum feasible H**.
- For fixed H, u_i must lie in [max(0, H−D_i), min(U_i, H)] with |u_i − u_{i+1}| ≤ X. Standard interval propagation gives feasibility: maintain lo_j = max_{i≤j}(A_i − X(j−i)), hi_j = min_{i≤j}(B_i + X(j−i)), feasible iff lo_j ≤ hi_j for all j.
- Rewriting: with A_i = max(0,H−D_i)+X·i and B_i = min(U_i,H)−X·i, feasibility ⟺ cummax(A)[j] − cummin(B)[j] ≤ 2Xj for all j — fully vectorizable.
- Feasibility is monotone in H (a feasible solution for H gives one for H−1 by u_i' = max(0, u_i−1), d_i' adjusted), so binary search on H ∈ [0, min(U_i+D_i)].

Verified against all four samples (sample 1 gives H=4 → cost 31−16=15; sample 3 gives H=2 → 6e9+2−8; sample 4 gives H=380 → 15160−5700=9460). Overflow is safe in int64 (values ~4×10^14).
