=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem: the minimal subtree containing all marked vertices in a tree is the union of paths between marked vertex pairs. 

**Key insight:** Root the tree at a marked vertex `r = V_1`. Then a vertex `u` belongs to the Steiner tree iff `cnt[u] >= 1`, where `cnt[u]` is the number of marked vertices in `u`'s subtree.

**Why:** If `u ≠ r` has a marked vertex `m` in its subtree, the path from `m` to `r` (marked) passes through `u` (u is an ancestor of m), so `u` is required. Conversely, any vertex on a path between marked vertices `a, b` is an ancestor of `a` or of `b` (path goes a→LCA(a,b)→b), so it contains a marked vertex in its subtree. And `r` itself is marked. So the answer is the count of vertices with `cnt[u] >= 1`.

**Verification with sample 1:** root at 1; cnt values: 1→3, 2→1 (contains marked 5), 3→1 (marked), 5→1 (marked), 4,6,7→0. Count of positive = 4. ✓ Sample 2 gives all 4. ✓ Sample 3 gives just vertex 1. ✓

**Implementation:** iterative DFS (avoid recursion limits for N=2×10⁵), then process vertices in reverse order accumulating `cnt` to parents, counting vertices with `cnt >= 1`. O(N) total.

Edge cases: N=1, K=1 → answer 1 (single vertex, no edges — loop over edges just doesn't execute; order=[1], cnt[1]=1). K=N → all vertices counted.

Recheck of the accumulation loop: reversed DFS pre-order guarantees children are processed before their parent, so `cnt[u]` is complete when checked, and pushing to parent happens after the child's full accumulation. Correct.
