Looking at this problem:

**Key insight**: The answer for `A_i` is the largest `g` such that `g | A_i` and at least `K` elements of `A` are divisible by `g` (if we can pick K multiples of g including A_i, gcd is a multiple of g, so ≥ g; conversely the achieved gcd itself satisfies both conditions).

**Plan**: 
1. `cnt = bincount(A)`, compute `f[g]` = count of multiples of g via harmonic sieve.
2. `ok[g] = f[g] >= K`, then `mx[v]` = max qualifying divisor of v, answer = `mx[A_i]`.

For Python speed with M up to 10^6, I avoid ~10^6 per-g numpy calls by batching: for g in range `(M//(k+1), M//k]`, each g has exactly k multiples ≤ M, so range k costs k vectorized ops (`Σk ≈ T²/2`), and small g (≤ M//(T+1)) use the plain strided loop. Balance gives T ≈ (2M)^(1/3) ≈ 126, so only ~16k numpy calls total per sieve. For the divisor-max sieve, within one op indices `j*gs` are distinct (injective in g for fixed j), so fancy-index `mx[idx] = np.maximum(mx[idx], gs)` is safe, and separate ops compose correctly via max (order-independent).
