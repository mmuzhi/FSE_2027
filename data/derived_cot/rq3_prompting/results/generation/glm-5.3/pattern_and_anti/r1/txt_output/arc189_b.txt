**Key insight:** Consider gaps $d_j = x_{j+1} - x_j$. For an operation with index $i$: the two middle pieces get reflected about $M = (x_i + x_{i+3})/2$. The new sorted positions within the window become $x_i,\ 2M - x_{i+2},\ 2M - x_{i+1},\ x_{i+3}$ (strictly inside the interval, so no collisions and other pieces unaffected). The three gaps $(a,b,c) = (d_i, d_{i+1}, d_{i+2})$ become $(c, b, a)$ — i.e., **the operation simply swaps gaps $d_i \leftrightarrow d_{i+2}$**.

Swaps of positions at distance 2 generate all permutations *within* odd-indexed gaps and *within* even-indexed gaps (adjacent transpositions exist for every $i \le N-3$).

Since $\sum_k x_k = N\cdot X_1 + \sum_j (N-j)\, d_j$, and $X_1$ (leftmost) is invariant, we minimize by the rearrangement inequality: within each parity class, pair the largest gaps with the smallest weights. Weights at positions $j = 1,3,5,\dots$ are $N-1, N-3, \dots$ (descending), so place gaps sorted **ascending** at positions in increasing order (same for even positions, weights $N-2, N-4, \dots$).
