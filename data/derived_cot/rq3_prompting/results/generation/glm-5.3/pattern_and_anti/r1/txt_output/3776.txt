Looking at this problem:

- Each pairing operation removes 2 of the current first three elements; exactly one "survivor" from the window carries forward (after the first op), and fresh elements enter the window two at a time (indices advance by 2 each operation).
- State: `(s, k)` = survivor at index `s`, fresh elements start at index `k` (window is `{s, k, k+1}`). Transitions: remove `{k,k+1}` (survivor stays), remove `{s,k}` (survivor becomes `k+1`), remove `{s,k+1}` (survivor becomes `k`), each advancing `k` by 2.
- Base: fewer than 3 remaining (`k >= n` → cost `nums[s]`; `k == n-1` → `max(nums[s], nums[n-1])`).
- Only odd `k >= 3` are reachable, giving O(n²/4) states — fine for n=1000.
