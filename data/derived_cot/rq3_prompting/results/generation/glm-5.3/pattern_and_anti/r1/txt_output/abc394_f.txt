Looking at this problem:

**Key facts:**
- An alkane with $c$ degree-4 vertices has exactly $l = 2c+2$ leaves (from $4c + l = 2(c+l-1)$), so total vertices $= 3c+2$. Maximizing vertices ⇔ maximizing $c$ (count of degree-4 vertices).
- The subgraph must be a connected subtree. Root $T$ at 1. For a connected subgraph, its topmost vertex $r$ has degree = number of included children ∈ {1, 4}; any other included vertex $u$ (parent edge included) must have exactly 0 or 3 included children.

**DP (bottom-up):** For vertex $u$ whose parent edge is included ("branch" at $u$):
- $g_3[u]$ = max degree-4 count with exactly 3 children branches = $1 + (\text{top-3 of } h[\text{child}])$ (impossible if < 3 children).
- $h[u] = \max(0, g_3[u])$ — $h = 0$ means child used as a bare leaf.

**Root candidates:** at vertex $v$: with 1 child (a leaf $v$): $\max g_3[\text{child}]$ (must be ≥1 to have any degree-4 vertex); with 4 children: $1 + \text{top-4 } h$ values. Answer $= 3\cdot\text{ans}+2$ if best $c \ge 1$, else $-1$.
