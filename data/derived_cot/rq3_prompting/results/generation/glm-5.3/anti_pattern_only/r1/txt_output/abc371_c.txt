Looking at this problem:

- We can only modify H, and each toggle of pair (i,j) costs A[i][j]. Toggling the same pair twice is never useful (positive costs), so the minimum cost to turn H into a specific target graph T is the sum of A over pairs where H and T differ.
- The valid targets are exactly the graphs T_P for permutations P, where edge (P_i, P_j) exists in T_P iff edge (i,j) exists in G.
- So the answer is min over all permutations P of Σ_{i<j} A[P_i][P_j] · [G has edge (i,j) XOR H has edge (P_i,P_j)].

With N ≤ 8, there are at most 8! = 40320 permutations × 28 pairs — trivial to brute force.
