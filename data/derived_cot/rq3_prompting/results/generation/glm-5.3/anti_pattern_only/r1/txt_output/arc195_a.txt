Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** Find the leftmost (greedy) match of B in A and the rightmost (greedy) match of B in A. 

- The leftmost greedy match L satisfies l_k ≤ x_k for all k, for *any* match X (by induction: l_1 is the first occurrence of B_1, and each subsequent l_k is the earliest valid continuation).
- Symmetrically, the rightmost greedy match R satisfies r_k ≥ x_k for all k, for any match X.

So if there are two distinct matches X ≠ Y, then at the first index where they differ, one has a strictly smaller position, forcing l_k < r_k for that k, hence L ≠ R. Conversely, if L ≠ R, they are themselves two distinct matches.

Therefore: answer is "Yes" iff a match exists AND leftmost match ≠ rightmost match.
