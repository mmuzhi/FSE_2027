You will be given a competitive programming problem.
There is a grid of N^2 squares with N rows and N columns.
Let (i,j) denote the square at the i-th row from the top (1\leq i\leq N) and j-th column from the left (1\leq j\leq N).
Each square is either empty or has a piece placed on it.
There are M pieces placed on the grid, and the k-th (1\leq k\leq M) piece is placed on square (a_k,b_k).
You want to place your piece on an empty square in such a way that it cannot be captured by any of the existing pieces.
A piece placed on square (i,j) can capture pieces that satisfy any of the following conditions:

- Placed on square (i+2,j+1)
- Placed on square (i+1,j+2)
- Placed on square (i-1,j+2)
- Placed on square (i-2,j+1)
- Placed on square (i-2,j-1)
- Placed on square (i-1,j-2)
- Placed on square (i+1,j-2)
- Placed on square (i+2,j-1)

Here, conditions involving non-existent squares are considered to never be satisfied.
For example, a piece placed on square (4,4) can capture pieces placed on the squares shown in blue in the following figure:

How many squares can you place your piece on?

The input is given from Standard Input in the following format:

a_1 b_1

Output

- 1\leq N\leq10^9
- 1\leq M\leq2\times10^5

Looking at this problem:

- The answer is N² minus the number of distinct squares that are either **occupied** by a piece or **attacked** (knight move) by some piece.
- Each piece contributes at most 9 candidate squares (itself + 8 knight targets), so at most 9M ≈ 1.8×10⁶ candidates. We just need to count distinct valid ones.
- Since coordinates go up to 10⁹, encode each square as `i * 2³⁰ + j` (injective since j ≤ 10⁹ < 2³⁰) and use a set.

**Verification with samples:**
- Sample 1: 6 occupied + 20 distinct attacked squares (no overlap) = 26 unusable → 64 − 26 = 38 ✓
- Sample 2: only (1,1), (2,3), (3,2) unusable → 10¹⁸ − 3 ✓
- Sample 3: manually computed 10 occupied + 54 attacked − 2 overlap = 62 unusable → 400 − 62 = 338 ✓

The implementation uses a single list comprehension over (piece × move) pairs (including delta (0,0) for the piece itself, which always passes the bounds check since pieces are on the grid), filters out-of-bounds targets, then takes the set size.

