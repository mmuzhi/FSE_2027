=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- A cycle containing vertex 1 starts at 1 and returns to 1 via directed edges.
- Any such cycle ends with an edge (u, 1), where the prefix is a walk from 1 to u. The minimum is achieved by taking a **shortest path** from 1 to u (BFS), then the edge u → 1.
- Since BFS shortest paths are simple and never revisit vertex 1 in the middle, concatenating a shortest path 1→...→u with edge u→1 forms a valid simple cycle. So the answer is `min over all edges (u,1) of dist(u) + 1`, where `dist` is BFS distance from vertex 1. If no incoming edge to 1 has a reachable source, answer is -1.

Verification with sample 3: BFS from 1 gives dist[5]=1, dist[4]=2, dist[2]=3, dist[6]=4. Incoming edges to 1 are from 6 (cycle length 5) and 2 (cycle length 4) → answer 4. ✓

Samples 1 (answer 3) and 2 (no incoming edge to 1 → -1) also check out.

Complexity: O(N + M), well within limits.
