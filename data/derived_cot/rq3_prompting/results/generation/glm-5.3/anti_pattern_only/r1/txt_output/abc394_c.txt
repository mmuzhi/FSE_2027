Looking at this problem:

- The operation `WA` → `AC` can be viewed as: an `A` "moves" one step left into a `W`, leaving a `C` behind (the `W` position becomes `A`, the old `A` position becomes `C`).
- Each `A` keeps moving left through its immediately preceding maximal run of `W`'s, stopping when the character before it is not `W`. Nothing else ever changes: `C`'s and other letters are never part of `WA`, and `W`'s only disappear.
- Two different `A`'s have disjoint preceding `W`-runs (a run of `W`'s belongs only to the `A` immediately following it), so blocks transform independently: `W^k A` (k≥1) becomes `A C^k`. `W`-runs not followed by an `A` stay unchanged, as do all other characters.

So a single left-to-right scan suffices: keep a counter of consecutive `W`'s; on seeing `A`, emit `A` followed by that many `C`'s and reset; on any other character, flush pending `W`'s then the character; flush remaining `W`'s at the end.

Verification with samples:
- `WACWA` → `AC` + `C` + `AC` = `ACCAC` ✓
- `WWA` → `ACC` ✓  
- `WWWWW` → `WWWWW` ✓
