=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

We need $\sum_{1\le l\le r\le N}\left(\sum_{i=l}^r A_i\right)^K \bmod 998244353$.

Using prefix sums $S_r = A_1+\dots+A_r$ (with $S_0=0$), the answer is $\sum_{0\le j<r\le N}(S_r-S_j)^K$. Expanding binomially:

$$\sum_{t=0}^K \binom{K}{t}(-1)^t \sum_{r=1}^N S_r^{K-t}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{r-1} S_j^t\Big)$$

So for each exponent $e\in[0,K]$ we precompute $S_i^e$ for all $i$, plus prefix sums of those arrays. Then for each $t$ we take a dot product. This is $O(NK)$ total.

**Verification on sample 1** ($N=3,K=2$, $A=[3,1,2]$, $S=[0,3,4,6]$): powers rows give $t=0$: $149$, $t=1$: $-2\cdot54$, $t=2$: $+34$ → $149-108+34=75$ ✓. Sample 2 ($S_1=0$) gives $0$ ✓.

For safety of intermediate values: numpy `int64` products of two values $<998244353$ fit ($<10^{18}$), cumsums of $2\times10^5$ such values fit ($<2\times10^{14}$). A pure-Python fallback (using `accumulate`/`map(mul,...)`) is included in case numpy is unavailable.
