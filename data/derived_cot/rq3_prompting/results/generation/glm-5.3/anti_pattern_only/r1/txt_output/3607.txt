Looking at this problem: dividing `x` by its greatest proper divisor (`x / spf(x)`) yields exactly `spf(x)` — the smallest prime factor of `x`. After that, the value is prime (or 1), and dividing a prime by its greatest proper divisor (which is 1) changes nothing. So each element can only ever be `x` or `spf(x)`.

Thus we greedily scan right-to-left: keep the current element if it's ≤ the chosen value to its right (cost 0, and gives the most room for the left); otherwise it must drop to its smallest prime factor (cost 1) — if even that exceeds the limit, return -1. Precompute smallest prime factors with a sieve (assigning larger primes first so smaller primes overwrite, yielding the smallest prime factor in each slot).

Verified against all three examples: `[25,7]` → 1, `[7,7,6]` → -1 (7 is stuck, 6→2 still < 7), `[1,1,1,1]` → 0.
