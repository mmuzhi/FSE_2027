Looking at this problem:

- f(x,y) is the bottleneck distance: the smallest threshold t such that x and y are connected using only edges of weight ≤ t (Kruskal-style connectivity).
- **Key insight**: Process edges in increasing weight order (Kruskal). When two components with counts (a₁,b₁) and (a₂,b₂) of unmatched-relevant A/B vertices merge at weight w, we can match `min(a₁+a₂, b₁+b₂) − min(a₁,b₁) − min(a₂,b₂)` new pairs at cost exactly w (any unmatched A and unmatched B in the same new component but different old components have f = w).

**Optimality proof**: For any threshold t, any matching has at most Σ_c min(a_c, b_c) pairs with cost ≤ t (sum over t-components). The greedy achieves exactly this bound simultaneously for every t (shown by induction: after processing a component, unmatched A = max(0, a−b), unmatched B = max(0, b−a), so matched = min(a,b)). Since total cost = Σ_t (K − pairs(≤t)), maximizing pairs(≤t) for all t minimizes the total.

Verified on both samples (gives 8 and 3). This is just Kruskal + DSU with per-component A/B counts — O(M log M).
