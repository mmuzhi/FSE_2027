import sys

def main():
    data = sys.stdin.buffer.read().split()
    n = int(data[0])
    xs = list(map(int, data[1:1+n]))
    gaps = [xs[j+1] - xs[j] for j in range(n-1)]
    
    total = n * xs[0]
    
    # odd 1-based positions: indices 0, 2, 4, ... in gaps
    odd = sorted(gaps[0::2])
    m = len(odd)
    # weights for positions 1,3,...,2m-1: n-1, n-3, ..., n-(2m-1), descending
    for k in range(m):
        total += odd[k] * (n - (2*k + 1))
    
    # even 1-based positions: indices 1, 3, 5, ... in gaps
    even = sorted(gaps[1::2])
    m = len(even)
    # weights for positions 2,4,...,2m: n-2, n-4, ..., descending
    for k in range(m):
        total += even[k] * (n - (2*k + 2))
    
    print(total)

main()

Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** Consider the sorted positions $p_1 < p_2 < \cdots < p_N$ and gaps $d_j = p_{j+1} - p_j$. The operation on window $(p_i, p_{i+1}, p_{i+2}, p_{i+3})$ replaces the middle two with $p_i + p_{i+3} - p_{i+2}$ and $p_i + p_{i+3} - p_{i+1}$ (both stay strictly between $p_i$ and $p_{i+3}$). Computing the new gaps shows this operation simply **swaps $d_i$ and $d_{i+2}$**, leaving all other gaps (including $d_{i+1}$) unchanged.

Thus gaps at odd positions can be arbitrarily permuted among odd positions, and even among even positions (adjacent transpositions within each parity class generate the full symmetric group).

The sum is $N \cdot p_1 + \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} (N-j)\,d_j$, where $p_1 = X_1$ is invariant. To minimize, by the rearrangement inequality, within each parity class assign the **largest gaps to the smallest weights** (smallest weights are at the largest positions).
