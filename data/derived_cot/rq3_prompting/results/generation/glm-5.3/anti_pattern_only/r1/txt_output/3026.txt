=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- Numbers `x` and `target - x` can't both be in the array (when `x < target - x`). For each such pair, taking the smaller side is always at least as good (an exchange argument: swapping a large complement for its small partner keeps the array beautiful and reduces the sum).
- Numbers `1..floor(target/2)` are mutually safe: any two distinct of them sum to less than `target` (and `target/2` is a self-complement, fine as a single distinct value). This gives a "cheap pool" of `m = target // 2` numbers.
- Numbers `>= target` conflict with nothing.

So the optimal construction: take `min(n, m)` smallest cheap numbers, then fill the rest with `target, target+1, ...`.

- If `n <= m`: sum = `n(n+1)/2`.
- Else with `rem = n - m`: sum = `m(m+1)/2 + rem*target + rem*(rem-1)/2`.

Verification with examples:
- `n=2, target=3`: `m=1`, rem=1 → `1 + 3 = 4` ✓
- `n=3, target=3`: `m=1`, rem=2 → `1 + 6 + 1 = 8` ✓
- `n=1, target=1`: `m=0`, rem=1 → `0 + 1 + 0 = 1` ✓

Python big integers handle the arithmetic; take mod at the end.
