Looking at this problem:

**Structure**: Edge $i$ connects $i$ and $\max(i-N,0)$, so vertex 0 has children $1..N$, and each vertex $v\ge 1$ has exactly one child $v+N$. The tree is a **spider**: $N$ legs (paths) of length $M$ attached to the root.

**Excursion decomposition**: Between visits to the root, the walk makes an "excursion" into a uniformly random leg (an independent SRW on path $0..M$ starting at depth 1, ending at root). Each excursion independently reaches depth $M$ (hits $M$ before $0$) with probability $1/M$ (gambler's ruin). The process ends mid-excursion at the first arrival at depth $M$ of the last unfinished leg.

- $E[D]$ (excursion duration) $= 1 + h_{1\to0} = 1 + (2M-1) = 2M$.
- $t^{end}$ (number of excursions until every leg has had a "successful" excursion) is coupon collection: each excursion gives a new coupon w.p. $\frac{N-k}{NM}$, so $E[t^{end}] = NM H_N$ where $H_N=\sum_{i=1}^N 1/i$.
- By Wald ($t^{end}$ is a stopping time w.r.t. excursion data, $D_t$ i.i.d. independent of the past): $E[\sum_{t\le t^{end}} D_t] = E[D]\,E[t^{end}]$.
- The final excursion is truncated: subtract the expected tail from first hit of $M$ back to root, which is $h_{M\to 0}=M^2$.

So $E[T] = 2M\cdot NM H_N - M^2 = M^2(2NH_N - 1)$.
