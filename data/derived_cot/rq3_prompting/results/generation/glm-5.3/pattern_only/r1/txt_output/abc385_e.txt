=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- A snowflake tree keeps: a center `c`, `x` neighbors of `c` (middle vertices), and for each middle `v`, `y` leaves chosen among `v`'s other neighbors. Since we delete vertices freely, a middle `v` can hold up to `deg(v) - 1` leaves.
- For a fixed center `c`, each neighbor `v` with `cap = deg(v) - 1 ≥ 1` can serve as a middle contributing `1 + y` vertices for any `1 ≤ y ≤ cap`. Sorting caps descending and trying every count `x` with `y = caps[x-1]` (the min cap among the top `x`) covers all optima, since for fixed `y` we take as many middles as possible.
- Leaf sets of different middles are disjoint (a shared neighbor would create a cycle in the tree), and leaves' other neighbors are simply deleted, so the kept set induces exactly the snowflake.

Thus answer = `N - max over centers c and x of (1 + x*(1 + caps[x-1]))`. Total work is O(sum of degrees · log) = O(N log N).

Verified against all three samples (answers 1, 0, 3). Edge case handled: caps equal to 0 (neighbors of degree 1) can't be middles since y ≥ 1 — break when hitting 0 in the sorted-descending list.
