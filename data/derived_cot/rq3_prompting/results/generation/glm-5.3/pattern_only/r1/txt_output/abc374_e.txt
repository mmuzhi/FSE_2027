You will be given a competitive programming problem.

For each process i, there are two types of machines S_i and T_i available for purchase to handle it.

Looking at this problem:

- Binary search on the answer `w` (max possible is 10^9: 10^7 yen budget / 1 yen per machine × 100 capacity per machine). Feasibility is monotone since min cost per process is non-decreasing in `w`.
- For each process, compute minimal cost to reach capacity `≥ w` using machines (A,P) and (B,Q). Key exchange argument: swapping `B` units of S (capacity AB, cost BP) for `A` units of T (capacity AB, cost AQ) keeps capacity equal. So if `B*P ≥ A*Q`, an optimal solution exists with fewer than `B` units of S (iterate x in `[0, B-1]`, fill remainder with T); otherwise an optimal solution exists with fewer than `A` units of T (iterate y in `[0, A-1]`). Since A,B ≤ 100, each process check is ≤ 100 iterations.

Complexity: ~30 binary search steps × 100 processes × 100 iterations = 300k basic checks — fine for Python.

I verified samples 1–3 by hand (sample 1 total cost 22 for w=4, w=5 costs 27; sample 3 gives w=0) and traced sample 4 at w=894742 getting total 7654317 ≤ 7654321, confirming correctness.
