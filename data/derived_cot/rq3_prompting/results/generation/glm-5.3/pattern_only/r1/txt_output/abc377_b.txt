=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** A piece at (i,j) captures everything in row i and column j. So our piece at (r,c) is safe if and only if:
1. Square (r,c) is empty
2. No existing piece is in row r
3. No existing piece is in column c

**Approach:** Compute which rows contain at least one `#` and which columns contain at least one `#`. Then count empty squares whose row has no piece AND whose column has no piece. (Note: a row with no pieces is entirely empty, so this count equals free_rows × free_cols, but a direct per-square check is simplest and safest.)

**Verification on samples:**
- Sample 1: rows 6,7 have no pieces; columns 6,7 have no pieces → squares (6,6),(6,7),(7,6),(7,7) → 4 ✓
- Sample 2: no pieces at all → 64 ✓
- Sample 3: rows 4,6 free; columns with pieces are {2,3,4,5,6,8}, so columns 1,7 free → (4,1),(4,7),(6,1),(6,7) → 4 ✓ (I initially miscounted row 5's second piece as column 7; it's actually column 8, which still yields free columns 1 and 7, matching the expected output.)

**Edge cases checked:** all-empty board (64), fully occupied rows/columns (contributes 0). Input parsing strips whitespace/line endings robustly.

Complexity is O(64) — trivial.
