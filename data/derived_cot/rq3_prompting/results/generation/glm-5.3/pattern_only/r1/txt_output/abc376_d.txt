=== ANSWER ===
**Analysis:**

A cycle containing vertex 1 is a closed walk 1 → ... → 1. The shortest closed walk from 1 back to 1 is always a simple cycle (if any vertex repeated, we could shortcut it using an existing edge, contradicting minimality). Therefore the answer is:

$$\min_{(u, 1) \in E} \big(\text{dist}(1, u) + 1\big)$$

where dist is the shortest-path distance from vertex 1 (BFS, unweighted graph). If no edge into 1 has a reachable tail, the answer is -1.

**Verification on samples:**
- Sample 1: dist = [0,1,2], edge (3,1) → 2+1 = 3 ✓
- Sample 2: no edge into 1 → -1 ✓
- Sample 3: dist[6]=4 (1→5→4→2→6), dist[2]=3; edges into 1: (6,1)→5, (2,1)→4; min = 4 ✓

Complexity: O(N + M), well within limits.
