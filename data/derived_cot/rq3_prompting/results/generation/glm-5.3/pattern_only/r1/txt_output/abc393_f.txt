=== ANSWER ===
**Key insight:** Process the array left-to-right maintaining the patience-sorting "tails" array `T`, where `T[k]` = minimum possible tail of a strictly increasing subsequence of length `k` in the prefix. `T` stays strictly increasing, and updates are single point assignments or appends (never middle insertions).

**Query correctness:** For a query `(R, X)`, the answer is the largest `k` with `T_R[k] ≤ X`. Why: a strictly increasing subsequence has all elements ≤ X iff its tail ≤ X (earlier elements are strictly smaller than the tail), and `T_R[k]` is the minimum tail among length-`k` increasing subsequences. Since `T` is sorted, this count is just `bisect_right(T, X)` at time `R`.

**Plan:** Bucket queries by `R`, sweep `i = 1..N`, update `T` via `bisect_left` (assign or append), and answer all queries at their `R` with a `bisect_right`. Complexity `O((N+Q) log N)` with bisects running at C speed — no Fenwick tree needed since `T` is directly searchable and updates are O(1) assignments.

**Verification on samples:** Sample 1: `T` evolves `[2]→[2,4]→[1,4]→[1,3]→[1,3]`; queries give 2, 1, 2 ✓. Sample 2: e.g. after prefix 7 `T=[1,5,6,7]`, query `(7,8)`→4, `(7,3)`→1; after prefix 8 `T=[1,5,6,7,9]`, `(8,9)`→5, `(8,7)`→4; all eight match ✓.
