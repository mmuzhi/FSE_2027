There are N mochi (rice cakes), arranged in ascending order of size.
The size of the i-th mochi (1\leq i\leq N) is A_i.
Given two mochi A and B, with sizes a and b respectively, you can make one kagamimochi (a stacked rice cake) by placing mochi A on top of mochi B if and only if a is at most half of b.

- From the N mochi, choose 2K of them to form K pairs. For each pair, place one mochi on top of the other, to make K kagamimochi.

- All input values are integers.

Looking at this problem:

- We need max K such that we can pick K "top" mochi and K "bottom" mochi with each top ≤ half its bottom.
- Key theorem (via exchange argument): if any valid K-pairing exists, then taking the K smallest mochi as tops and K largest as bottoms, paired in sorted order, also works. So K is feasible ⟺ for all i < K: 2·A[i] ≤ A[N−K+i].
- Let j(i) = first index with A[j] ≥ 2·A[i] (binary search, since array sorted). Then feasibility of K ⟺ max_{i<K}(j(i)−i) ≤ N−K.
- Let d_i = j(i)−i and M(K) = prefix max of d. Then M(K)+K is strictly increasing in K, so feasible K's form a prefix — scan/break works. Cap K at N//2 (need 2K ≤ N).
