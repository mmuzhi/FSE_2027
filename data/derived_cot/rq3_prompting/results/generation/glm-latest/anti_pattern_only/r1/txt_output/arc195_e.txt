=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

- The distance between u and v is a sum of edge weights A_i over edges on the path, so by linearity, the answer is (N-1)! × Σ A_i · Pr[edge i on path(u,v)].
- Since parents are smaller labels, descendants of i are all > i. For u < v:
  - i > v: never on path (coefficient 0).
  - i = v: always on path (coefficient 1), since u < v can't be in v's subtree.
  - u < i < v: on path iff i is an ancestor of v; known probability 1/i for random recursive trees.
  - i = u: on path iff u is not an ancestor of v: probability 1 − 1/u = (u−1)/u.
  - 2 ≤ i < u: on path iff exactly one of u, v lies in subtree of i. The subtree membership of vertices > i forms an exchangeable Pólya-urn sequence (initial counts: 1 "in", i−1 "out"), so Pr[exactly one] = 2·(i−1)/(i(i+1)).

This gives an O(1) per query formula with two prefix sums:
Ans(u,v) = (N−1)! · [ A_v + A_u·(u−1)/u + Σ_{i=u+1}^{v−1} A_i/i + Σ_{i=2}^{u−1} 2(i−1)A_i/(i(i+1)) ]

I verified this against sample 1, sample 2, several brute-force enumerations (N=4), and exact hand computations of sample 3 queries (all match, e.g., query (3,8) → 55973424, (2,9) → 343265517, (2,5) → 496202632).

        total = (total + P[v - 1] - P[u] + R[u - 1]) % MOD
        out.append(total * F % MOD)

    sys.stdout.write('\n'.join(map(str, out)) + '\n')

main()
