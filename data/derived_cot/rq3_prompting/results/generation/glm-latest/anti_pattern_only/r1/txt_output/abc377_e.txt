Looking at this problem:

- The operation replaces P with P∘P (function squaring), so after K operations we have P^(2^K) as a function.
- Decompose the permutation into cycles. For a cycle (c_0, c_1, ..., c_{L-1}) where P(c_j) = c_{j+1}, applying P^(m) gives c_{(j+m) mod L}.
- So for each cycle of length L, compute r = 2^K mod L (fast modular exponentiation), and the answer for position c_j is c_{(j+r) mod L}.

Verification with sample 1: cycle (1,5,2,6,4) of length 5, r = 2³ mod 5 = 3, giving (6,1,3,2,4,5) — matches. Sample 3 also verified by hand (cycles of lengths 14, 11, 4 with r = 8, 9, 0 respectively produce the expected output).

Complexity: O(N) for cycle finding plus O(√N) modular exponentiations (cached by cycle length), easily fast enough for N ≤ 2×10⁵ and K ≤ 10¹⁸.
