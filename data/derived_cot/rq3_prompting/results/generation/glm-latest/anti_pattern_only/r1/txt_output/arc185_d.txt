Looking at this problem:

**Tree structure:** Edge $i$ connects $i$ and $\max(i-N,0)$, so vertex 0 has children $1..N$, and each vertex $j\ge 1$ has exactly one child $j+N$ (if $\le NM$). This is a "broom": root with $N$ chains of $M$ vertices each.

**Key observations:**
- The walk decomposes into i.i.d. *excursions* from the root: each picks a chain uniformly ($1/N$), then random-walks on $\{0,\dots,M\}$ starting at depth 1 until returning to root. Expected excursion length $= 1 + e_1$ where $e_i = i(2M-i)$, giving $2M$.
- An excursion completes a chain (reaches its bottom) with probability $1/M$ (gambler's ruin from depth 1).
- Painting doesn't affect dynamics, so this is just the cover time.
- Phase $c$ (at root with $c$ chains completed): each excursion completes a new chain with prob $p_c = \frac{N-c}{N}\cdot\frac{1}{M}$. With $S_c = E[L] + (1-p_c)S_c$, we get $S_c = \frac{2M}{p_c} = \frac{2NM^2}{N-c}$.
- The process ends at the $N$-th completion (at a chain bottom), before the final return to root. By strong Markov, expected return from bottom to root is $e_M = M^2$.
