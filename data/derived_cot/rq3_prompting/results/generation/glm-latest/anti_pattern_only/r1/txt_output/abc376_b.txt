Looking at this problem:

- The two hands start at parts 1 (left) and 2 (right).
- For each instruction, only the specified hand moves, while the other hand stays fixed. The moving hand cannot step onto the fixed hand's part.
- Key observation: given the moving hand at position `s`, target `T`, and fixed hand at `f` (with `T ≠ f`, guaranteed), the fixed hand lies on exactly one of the two arcs from `s` to `T`. So exactly one direction around the ring is passable, and the minimal path is unique and forced.
- Since the state after each instruction is fully determined (moved hand at `T`, other unchanged), and taking detours never helps, the answer is simply the sum of these forced direct path lengths.

For each instruction:
- `cw = (T - s) mod N` is the clockwise distance.
- `df = (f - s) mod N` is the clockwise distance to the fixed hand.
- The clockwise path steps on parts at clockwise distances `1..cw` from `s`, so it's blocked iff `1 ≤ df ≤ cw`. If blocked, go counterclockwise with cost `(s - T) mod N`.
