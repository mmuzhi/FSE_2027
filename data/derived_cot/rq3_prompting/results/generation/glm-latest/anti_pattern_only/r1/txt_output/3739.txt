=== ANSWER ===
Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** By linearity of expectation/summation, swap the order: instead of iterating over arrangements, iterate over pairs of cells. For any unordered pair of distinct cells, the number of arrangements (k-subsets of the N = m·n cells) containing both cells is C(N-2, k-2). So:

Total = C(N-2, k-2) × Σ over unordered cell pairs of Manhattan distance.

**Computing the sum of distances over all cell pairs:** Split into row and column parts.

- Row part: pairs of cells in rows differing by d (there are m-d such row pairs, each giving n² cell pairs since columns are free): Σ_{d=1}^{m-1} d·(m-d)·n² = n²·m(m²-1)/6.
- Column part symmetrically: m²·n(n²-1)/6.

**Verification with examples:**
- m=2,n=2,k=2: C(2,0)=1, terms 4·1 + 4·1 = 8 ✓
- m=1,n=4,k=3: C(2,1)=2, terms 0 + 1·10 = 10, total 20 ✓

Also verified m=2,n=3 by brute-force enumeration of all 15 cell pairs (row part 9, column part 16).

The binomial is computed with factorials mod 10⁹+7 (N ≤ 10⁵ so this is fast). Since m·(m²−1) = (m−1)m(m+1) is always divisible by 6, the exact integer division is safe before taking mod.
