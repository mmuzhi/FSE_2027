Looking at this problem:

**Key insight:** X is good iff X = 2^a × b^2 with a ≥ 1, b ≥ 1. Analyzing the 2-adic valuation of X: all odd primes must have even exponents (they come from b²), and the exponent of 2 must be ≥ 1. 

Any good X can be uniquely written as:
- X = 2·(b)² if the exponent of 2 in X is odd (a odd, fold the rest into b),
- X = 4·(b)² if the exponent of 2 in X is even (≥ 2).

These two families are disjoint: if 2b₁² = 4b₂², then v₂(b₁²) is even while v₂(2b₂²) is odd — contradiction.

So the answer is:
- count of b with 2b² ≤ N, which is ⌊√(N/2)⌋ = isqrt(N//2)
- plus count of b with 4b² ≤ N, which is ⌊√(N/4)⌋ = isqrt(N//4)

**Verification with samples:**
- N=20: isqrt(10)=3 (gives 2,8,18) + isqrt(5)=2 (gives 4,16) = 5 ✓
- N=400: isqrt(200)=14 + isqrt(100)=10 = 24 ✓
