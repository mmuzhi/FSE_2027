Looking at this problem:

- A subarray `nums[l..r]` can be made non-decreasing with minimum cost = `Σ (max(nums[l..i]) - nums[i])` (each element must be raised at least to the running prefix max, and that's achievable).
- Reverse the array: the condition becomes — subarray `b[a..c]` can be made **non-increasing** with cost `Σ (max(b[j..c]) - b[j])` (suffix maxima). This is nice because removing the **leftmost** element just deletes one term (suffix maxima of remaining positions are unchanged), while extending the right end is handled with a monotonic stack of segments `(value, count)` where value = suffix max over that block.
- For fixed `a`, cost is non-decreasing in `c`; for fixed `c`, cost is non-increasing in `a`. So two pointers works: for each right end `c` (in reversed array), shrink left end `a` while cost > k; then all subarrays `[a..c]` are valid, adding `c - a + 1`.

Maintain the cost incrementally: when extending with `x`, pop all top segments with value ≤ `x` (their suffix max rises to `x`, cost += `(x-v)*cnt`); when shrinking, remove leftmost position (cost -= `bottom_value - b[a]`).
